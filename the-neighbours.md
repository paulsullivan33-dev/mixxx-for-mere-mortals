# The Neighbours

Mixxx is not the only way to DJ with
a computer, and pretending otherwise
would insult both you and the tools.
An honest map:

## Serato DJ

The club standard in much of the
world, especially for scratch and
hip-hop DJing. Free in its Lite
form, paid in Pro; it unlocks its
best self only with specific
licensed hardware, which is the
deal: the hardware *is* the dongle.
Superb vinyl control (it invented
the mainstream form), a library
system working DJs swear by, and a
price model where the software is
the cheap half. If you play on other
people's Serato setups, learn
Serato.

## Rekordbox

Pioneer's. Its library preparation —
analysed tracks on a USB stick,
plugged straight into the CDJs that
are the furniture of most club
booths — is the real product: DJs
who play venues prepare in Rekordbox
because the booth demands it. Also a
full DJ program in its own right,
with performance features unlocked
by Pioneer hardware and
subscriptions creeping around the
edges. Mixxx can read some of the
same world (its database has tables
for importing Rekordbox, Traktor and
Serato libraries — the lab saw them
in the schema), but it cannot write
a Rekordbox USB the booth will love.

## Traktor

Native Instruments' program: strong
effects, remix decks for live
remixing, deep ties to NI's own
controllers. A design-led tool that
some DJs find inspiring and others
find opinionated. Paid, hardware-
friendly, and its timecode records
work in Mixxx (chapter 12), which
tells you something about how the
ecosystem's pieces interlock.

## VirtualDJ

The populist: free for home use with
a mouse, endlessly skinnable,
supports nearly every controller
ever made, and adds video and
karaoke to the DJ brief. Stems
separation — splitting a playing
track into vocal/instrumental live —
arrived here early. Working DJs in
mobile and wedding work rate it
higher than club folklore admits.

## And the non-DJ neighbours

Most music software is not DJ
software. **Audacity** edits audio
files — cutting, cleaning,
converting — and does not play sets;
**ffmpeg** is the command-line
engine under half the audio world
(it manufactured this guide's
fixtures). Media servers like
**Jellyfin** and **Navidrome** —
both in this series — organise a
collection for *listening*, the way
Mixxx organises one for *playing*:
the same files, an entirely
different question asked of them.
