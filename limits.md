# Limits

What Mixxx cannot do, or does not
do, stated plainly.

- **It cannot listen for you.** The
  lab's central fact is also yours:
  Mixxx's numbers — BPM, key, grids —
  are measurements by algorithm, and
  they are sometimes confidently
  wrong (86.25 BPM for a test tone
  with no beat in it, chapter 6). The
  software has no taste and no doubt.
- **No real-time stem separation.**
  Unlike some neighbours, Mixxx 2.4
  does not split a playing track into
  vocals and instruments on the fly.
  Acapella tricks need files that
  arrive pre-split.
- **Analysis assumes a grid.**
  Music that drifts — live drummers,
  rubato classical, many vinyl rips —
  breaks its favourite assumption.
  Constant-BPM reanalysis and hand
  edits help at the margins; the
  manual skill of chapter 8 is the
  real answer.
- **Keys are a detector's opinion.**
  On music with actual notes it is
  often right (C, for the lab's
  arpeggio); on drums it prints
  something anyway. Treat the key
  column as a suggestion with a
  known failure mode.
- **It is not a booth.** Mixxx
  cannot prepare the Rekordbox USB
  sticks that club CDJs expect, and
  its library, for all its virtues,
  doesn't travel into the other
  ecosystems' hardware. It can
  *import* their libraries' tables;
  it does not replace them.
- **Hardware is only as mapped.**
  294 controller mappings is a lot;
  the newest controller, or the one
  with the unusual screen, may not
  be among them, and mapping is work.
- **Sound is someone else's job.**
  Mixxx has no opinion about your
  speakers and cannot fix a bad
  interface — it can only fail to
  find one at all, as the lab
  demonstrated. Latency (the delay
  between touching a control and
  hearing it) is set by your audio
  device and drivers, not by any
  knob in this program; on a bad
  setup, scratching will always feel
  drunk.
- **Broadcasting is basic.** Mixxx
  is a stream *source*, one profile
  at a time, to one server at a
  time. Station automation,
  scheduling and listener statistics
  live server-side, in tools like
  Icecast and its bigger siblings.
- **Nothing is backed up for you.**
  Your library database — years of
  cues, crates and counts — is one
  file, and chapter 5 tells you to
  copy it because nobody else will.
