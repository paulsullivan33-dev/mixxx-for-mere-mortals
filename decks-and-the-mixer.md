# Decks and the Mixer

This chapter is a tour of the
controls, in the order your hands
meet them. The lab could see all of
them on screen — the screenshots in
`mixxx-lab/` show the window as it
ran — and it could not hear any of
them; what each control *does to the
sound* is described from the Mixxx
manual, and labelled as such in
`criteria.md`.

## Loading a track

Drag a track from the library onto a
deck, double-click it, or right-click
and use *Load to*. The deck shows the
track's title, its waveform twice —
a small overview of the whole song
and a large scrolling close-up of
where you are — and its measured BPM
beside the original: if you change
the deck's tempo, Mixxx shows both,
so you always know how far the track
has been pushed from itself.

## The transport row

- **Play/Pause** — starts and stops.
- **CUE** — the DJ's home key. While
  paused, it sets the cue point where
  you are; while playing, it jumps
  back to that point and pauses. Sets
  are built on it: cue the first beat
  of the chorus, and the track is a
  loaded spring.
- **Hotcues 1–8** — chapter 9; the
  same trick, eight more places.
- The **spinny** — the turning disc.
  Drag it to nudge the track forward
  or back by a hair, or to scratch.
  Its speed *is* the track's speed.

## Tempo

The vertical slider beside each deck
changes playback speed — and, unless
you engage **key lock**, pitch with
it. ±8% is the classic turntable
range; Mixxx allows wider. Key lock
holds the song's key while the tempo
moves, which is what makes blending
a 118 BPM track into a 122 BPM set
possible without chipmunk vocals.
The **SYNC** button above the slider
is chapter 8.

## The channel strip

From deck to audience, each channel
runs through:

- **Gain** — trims the track's level
  into the mixer (ReplayGain can set
  this for you, chapter 6).
- **High / Mid / Low EQ** — three
  knobs that turn down bands of the
  sound. The standard blend: as the
  new track enters, turn the old
  track's *Low* down and the new
  one's up, so two basslines never
  play at once.
- **Filter** — one knob, a sweep:
  left strips the highs away until
  the track sounds underwater; right
  strips the lows until it's all
  fizz. A whole transition can be
  just this knob turning.
- **Channel fader** — the channel's
  final volume.
- The little **headphone button** —
  sends this channel to your
  headphones *instead of* (not as
  well as) the main mix, so you can
  cue the next track in private.

## The middle

The mixer's centre holds the
**crossfader** — left deck, right
deck, or both, according to its
position — the **MAIN** knob (the
audience's volume), the headphone
**MIX** knob (how much main mix vs.
cue signal your headphones carry)
and **BAL**ance. Above the decks,
the **REC** button records the main
output (chapter 11), and **ON AIR**
lights when a broadcast is live
(same chapter).

The meter bridges — two columns of
lights per the whole mix — are the
one part the lab *could* verify by
sight: with Auto DJ playing on its
fallback clock, the meters danced
green for tracks nobody could hear,
behind the dialog that later asked
*A deck is currently playing. Exit
Mixxx?* The engine mixes whether or
not anyone is listening. That is
rather the point of the machine.
